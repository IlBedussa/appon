If you let a home in Israel, you are a landlord under a set of rules that changed significantly in 2017. That year the Knesset amended the Rental and Borrowing Law of 1971 and added a dedicated chapter for residential leases, sections 25A to 25O. Most people call it the **Fair Rental Law** (חוק שכירות הוגנת).

For tenants it set minimum standards. For landlords, especially those who live abroad, it set deadlines and limits that your lease, your budget and your local support need to respect. This guide walks through the rules section by section, using the [consolidated law text on Nevo](https://www.nevo.co.il/law_html/law00/5149.htm) and the [amendment as published by the Knesset](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/20/law/20_lsr_389390.pdf). It is general information, not legal advice.

## When it started and what it covers

The amendment was passed on 17 July 2017 and published on 19 July 2017 ([Knesset](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/20/law/20_lsr_389390.pdf)). Commentators gave different start dates at the time. One law-firm article cited September 2017 ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/landlord-tenant-leases/620648/the-new-fair-rent-law-what-do-you-need-to-know)), while other sources refer to leases signed from December 2017 [DA VERIFICARE: exact commencement date and transitional rule for leases signed before it]. For any lease you sign today, assume it applies.

**What it does not cover (s. 25O):**

- Hotel apartments and other apartments let **for holiday purposes**.
- Student or worker dormitories, sheltered housing, and apartments under the Tenant Protection Law (protected tenancy).
- Leases of **up to three months** with no option to extend.
- Leases **longer than ten years** where the landlord has no right to end them earlier.
- Leases where the monthly rent exceeds **₪20,000**, a threshold updated every 1 January by the CPI.

Between close relatives (spouse, siblings, parents, descendants and their spouses), the parties may agree to depart from the mandatory provisions (s. 25O(b)).

## 1. Put it in writing (ss. 25B–25C)

A residential lease must be in writing and signed by both parties, and each must get a signed copy. A verbal lease is still valid, but it puts you on weak ground in any dispute. The Second Schedule lists what the lease must include:

- address and parties' details;
- a description of the apartment, furniture and equipment, including attached common areas;
- the term, any option to extend and its conditions, and any right to terminate;
- rent, payment dates and method;
- other payments the tenant must make;
- **known defects** that are not trivial, and anything nearby the landlord knows could seriously disturb use.

That last item matters for owners abroad. If you know about a recurring leak or upcoming construction next door, write it down. Disclosure protects you.

## 2. Hand over an apartment fit to live in (ss. 25E–25G)

The apartment must match what was agreed and be **fit for living**. Under the First Schedule, it is *not* fit if it lacks drainage and sewage disposal, an electrical or lighting system, ventilation and natural light openings with doors and windows (including a lockable main door), a drinking-water supply, or a partition between the toilet and the rest of the apartment, or if it poses an unreasonable risk to the tenant's safety or health. Handing over an unfit apartment counts as a breach of contract.

You must deliver on the agreed date, empty of other people and belongings (except what was agreed), and give the tenant **maintenance and operating instructions** for the apartment and its systems (s. 25G). Think boiler and solar heater, air conditioning, the alarm and the mamad ventilation system. A one-page guide in Hebrew and English saves arguments later.

## 3. Repairs: the 3-day and 30-day rules (s. 25H)

This is the section that most affects owners abroad:

- The **tenant** is responsible for defects caused by unreasonable use, and repairs them at their own cost unless agreed otherwise.
- The **landlord** must repair any other defect that is not trivial, within a reasonable time and **no later than 30 days** after the tenant's request.
- If the defect is **urgent**, meaning it prevents reasonable living in the apartment, the deadline is **3 days**.
- If the landlord misses the deadline, the tenant can use the general remedies of the law, which [Realta](https://realta.co.il/en/guides/tenant-rights/) summarises as repairing and deducting reasonable costs, or a proportional rent reduction.

Three days is not long when you are in another time zone and Shabbat falls in between. In practice you need someone in Israel with authority to approve urgent work up to a set amount, and trusted tradespeople on call. Lawyers advising non-resident landlords make the same point: the gap between the tenant's call and an approved repair is where problems escalate ([IsraelNonResident](https://israelnonresident.com/property/rental-management/hiring-property-manager-israel-non-resident-landlord/)).

## 4. Who pays what (s. 25I)

**The tenant pays:** rent; taxes on the occupier, including arnona; consumption charges such as water, electricity, gas and heating; and routine maintenance payments to the building committee or its management company.

**The tenant does not pay directly:**

- purchases or upgrades of fixed systems serving the apartment (unless the tenant asked for them);
- **building insurance** for the structure;
- the landlord's debts to third parties, **including a broker's fee where the broker acted for the landlord**.

If you use a broker to find tenants, budget for the fee yourself.

## 5. Deposits and guarantees (s. 25J)

- **Cap:** all guarantees that cost the tenant money together, such as a cash deposit, a bank guarantee or a guarantee from another licensed provider, may not exceed the **lower** of (1) the rent for one third of the lease term or (2) three months' rent. Personal guarantors and promissory notes are not counted, because they cost the tenant nothing ([Realta](https://realta.co.il/en/guides/tenant-rights/)). The "other licensed provider" category (credit providers, insurers) is a recent addition to the law text [DA VERIFICARE: date in force].
- **When you may use it:** unpaid rent, defects the tenant was responsible for and did not repair, unpaid arnona or utilities, and failure to vacate at the end of the lease.
- **Notice first:** you must tell the tenant a reasonable time in advance and give them a chance to fix the problem.
- **Return:** within **60 days** of getting the apartment back, or of the tenant's debts being settled if later, including any interest or returns earned on it.

## 6. Selling during a lease (s. 25K)

If you sell, you must notify the tenant in writing before handing the apartment to the buyer, with the buyer's details and contact information.

## 7. Renewals and early termination (ss. 25L–25M)

- Tell the tenant a **reasonable time before the end** whether you want to extend and on what terms.
- If **you** hold an option to extend, give notice **at least 90 days** before the end. You can only use it if the extension terms were set in the lease in advance.
- If the **tenant** holds the option, they must notify you at least 60 days before the end.
- A clause letting the landlord cancel **without cause** is void unless the tenant has the same right. Even then, the landlord must give **90 days'** notice and the tenant 60.

## 8. You cannot contract out (s. 25N)

Most of these provisions cannot be changed to the tenant's detriment, even if the tenant agrees. Old template leases from before 2017, or from abroad, often contain clauses that are now void. Have an Israeli lawyer review your template.

## What this means if you live abroad

- **Have a local decision-maker** who can approve urgent repairs within hours, not days.
- **Keep a written trail.** Tenant requests, quotes, invoices and photos protect you if a deposit dispute arises.
- **Calendar the deadlines:** 90 days before the lease ends for renewal decisions, and 60 days after move-out for the deposit.
- **Budget for the costs the law keeps with you:** building insurance, capital improvements and your own broker.
