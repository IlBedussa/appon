When you live abroad, a property manager is the person who turns "my apartment in Israel" from a recurring worry into a line on a monthly statement. But "property management" can mean very different things, and prices are rarely published. Here is what the job really involves, how fees are structured in Israel, and how to judge whether a quote is fair.

## What a property manager actually does

### For a long-term rental

A full-service manager typically covers:

1. **Marketing and tenant selection:** photos, listings, viewings, checking references and guarantors.
2. **The lease:** a contract that complies with the 2017 Fair Rental Law, signed within the authority you grant.
3. **Move-in:** inventory with photos, meter readings, and transferring arnona, water and electricity to the tenant.
4. **Rent collection:** monitoring payments, chasing arrears, and keeping you informed early.
5. **Maintenance:** taking the tenant's calls, getting quotes, coordinating trusted tradespeople, approving within your limit, and checking the work.
6. **Dealing with the vaad bayit and municipality.**
7. **Statements and transfers:** a monthly report of rent in and expenses out, and transfers of the net amount.
8. **Renewals and move-out:** renewal terms in time, final inspection, and deposit handling within the legal deadline.

Lawyers who advise non-resident landlords describe the same core scope ([IsraelNonResident](https://israelnonresident.com/property/rental-management/hiring-property-manager-israel-non-resident-landlord/)). Note that a manager does **not** automatically have authority to sell, mortgage or sign long-term commitments. That requires specific authority.

### For an empty or holiday home

Here the job is "care" rather than tenants: regular inspections (water, drains, damp, appliances), mail, bills and arnona, small repairs, and preparing the apartment before you arrive for the chagim.

## How managers in Israel charge

There is no regulated tariff. Published sources describe a few common models:

- **Percentage of rent** for long-term rentals: typically **5–10%** ([Meidahon](https://meidahon.co.il/real-estate/property-management)), **7–10%**, with central areas closer to 10% ([Dugrinet](https://dugrinet.co.il/cost-of-apartment-management-for-rent/)), or **8–10% + VAT** ([The Agency TLV](https://theagencytlv.com/property-management-fees-israel-2026/)). A law site focused on non-resident landlords cites **8–12% + VAT** ([IsraelNonResident](https://israelnonresident.com/property/rental-management/hiring-property-manager-israel-non-resident-landlord/)).
- **Monthly minimums:** some managers set a floor. One national operator publishes a ₪225 minimum ([Flamingo](https://flamingo-h.co.il/en/investors/pricing)), and Dugrinet cites typical minimums of ₪400–500.
- **Tenant-finding fee:** commonly **half to one month's rent** ([The Agency TLV](https://theagencytlv.com/property-management-fees-israel-2026/)).
- **Fixed monthly fee** for empty or holiday homes: The Agency TLV gives a market range of **₪500–1,500** a month, and one Jerusalem firm publishes packages at ₪1,250 and ₪2,500 + VAT ([Kaufman Realty](https://kaufmanrlty.com/property-management/)).
- **Maintenance coordination:** either included, or charged at **10–15%** of the job ([The Agency TLV](https://theagencytlv.com/property-management-fees-israel-2026/)).
- **Short-term rentals:** much higher, often **15–25%** plus cleaning, because the work is continuous.

Published prices change. Check the current terms on each provider's site [DA VERIFICARE: recheck third-party prices before publishing].

## Don't forget VAT

Israel's VAT rate went from 17% to **18% on 1 January 2025** ([Knesset](https://main.knesset.gov.il/EN/News/PressReleases/Pages/press12324w.aspx)). Management fees carry VAT, so **10% quoted becomes about 11.8% paid**, a point non-resident owners often miss ([IsraelNonResident](https://israelnonresident.com/property/rental-management/hiring-property-manager-israel-non-resident-landlord/)).

**Worked example (Jerusalem average rent):** CBS data republished by CEIC put the average rent in Jerusalem at ₪5,347 a month for the quarter ending June 2026 ([CEIC](https://www.ceicdata.com/en/israel/average-rental-price-dwellings/rental-prices-avg-jerusalem)).

## The tax angle

Whether the fee reduces your tax depends on your route. On the **ordinary (marginal) route** the management fee is a deductible expense. On the **10% route** and the **exemption route** it is not ([Bizportal](https://www.bizportal.co.il/guides/news/article/20039036); [IsraelNonResident](https://israelnonresident.com/property/rental-management/hiring-property-manager-israel-non-resident-landlord/)). Model it with your accountant.

## What "fair" really means: it's about transparency

A fair fee is not simply the lowest percentage. It is a price where you know exactly what you get and what you will never be charged. Watch for the costs that do not appear in the headline rate. Flamingo, for example, lists the renewal commissions, markups on contractors' invoices and exit penalties that some managers charge ([Flamingo](https://flamingo-h.co.il/en/investors/expertise/property-management-cost)):

- **Renewal fees** each time the same tenant extends.
- **Markups on repairs:** you pay the contractor's price plus a hidden percentage.
- **Fees while the apartment is empty** on a percentage plan.
- **Long lock-ins or exit penalties.**
- **"Extras"** for inspections, court appearances, or handling the deposit.

None of these is illegal in itself. What matters is that they are written down before you sign.

## Legal points to check

- **Brokerage requires a licence.** Charging a fee for matching landlord and tenant is regulated brokerage: the broker needs a valid licence and a signed written order with the prescribed details ([Realta](https://realta.co.il/en/guides/real-estate-agents/)). Ask for the licence number.
- **The landlord pays their own broker.** Under the Fair Rental Law, the tenant does not pay a broker who acted for the landlord ([s. 25I, consolidated law](https://www.nevo.co.il/law_html/law00/5149.htm)).
- **Your money should be kept separate.** Lawyers recommend a designated account for your rent, separate from the manager's own funds, with a fixed monthly transfer date ([IsraelNonResident](https://israelnonresident.com/property/rental-management/hiring-property-manager-israel-non-resident-landlord/)).

## Percentage or fixed fee: which model suits you?

- **Percentage of rent** aligns the manager's income with yours: if the apartment is empty, a fair percentage plan charges nothing. It suits long-term rentals where collection and tenant relations are the main work.
- **Fixed monthly fee** suits homes that are empty most of the year, where the work is inspections, bills and preparation, and does not depend on rent.
- **Hybrid set-ups** are common: a percentage while rented, switching to a fixed care fee if you take the apartment back for family use.

Whatever the model, compare the **total yearly cost including VAT and likely extras**, not the headline rate. A 7% plan with a renewal fee, repair markups and paid inspections can cost more than a 10% plan that includes them.

## Red flags

- No written agreement, or a one-paragraph engagement with no scope.
- Rent paid into a personal account, with no statements.
- Reluctance to show original contractor invoices.
- No licensed broker for tenant-finding.
- Vague answers on emergencies outside office hours.

## Ten questions to ask before you sign

1. Exactly what is included in the monthly fee, and what costs extra?
2. Is the fee charged when the apartment is empty?
3. Do you add any markup to contractors' invoices? Will I see the original invoice?
4. What can you spend without my approval, and how do I approve the rest?
5. How fast do you respond to an urgent repair, including on Fridays and before Shabbat?
6. How often will I get a statement, and when is the rent transferred?
7. Is my rent held in a separate account?
8. Who is the licensed broker for tenant-finding, and what is the licence number?
9. How often do you inspect, and do I get photos or video?
10. What is the notice period to end the contract, and are there exit fees?

## When you may not need one

If you live nearby, read Hebrew comfortably, have reliable tradespeople, and your tenant has been stable for years, self-management can work well. The case for a manager grows with distance, time zones, language barriers, a portfolio of more than one unit, or an apartment that sits empty between family visits.
