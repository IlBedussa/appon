For many owners abroad, the apartment in Israel really comes to life twice a year. Globes has noted that many foreign buyers in Jerusalem, a large share of them religious families from English- and French-speaking countries, come for **Tishrei and Pesach** ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-foreign-residents-dominate-jerusalem-city-center-housing-market-1001502805)). Arriving after months away to find a leaking boiler, a dead fridge or a cleaner who cancelled is a stressful start to the chag. A simple countdown prevents most of it.

## Mark the dates first

| Holiday | Starts (evening) | Ends |
|---|---|---|
| Chanukah 2026 | 4 December 2026 | 12 December 2026 |
| Pesach 2027 | 21 April 2027 | 28 April 2027 |
| Shavuot 2027 | 10 June 2027 | 11 June 2027 |
| Rosh Hashana 5788 | 1 October 2027 | 3 October 2027 |
| Yom Kippur | 10 October 2027 | 11 October 2027 |
| Sukkot & Shemini Atzeret | 15 October 2027 | 23 October 2027 |

Count back from your arrival date, not from the chag itself. If you land three days before Pesach, everything below needs to be done three days earlier than you think.

## 8 weeks before: inspect and book

**Get eyes on the apartment.** A walk-through with photos or a short video should cover:

- ceilings, walls under windows, and around the bathroom and kitchen for damp or mould;
- under sinks and around the toilet and washing machine connections for slow leaks;
- the water heater (dud) or solar system, by running the hot water;
- every air conditioner: power on, cooling and heating, filters, drainage;
- the electrical panel: no tripped breakers, and the residual-current device tested;
- the fridge and freezer, door seals, and the oven and hob;
- shutters (trisim), window locks and the front door lock;
- the mamad (safe room) or shelter access, including the ventilation filter system if fitted, and basic supplies (see Home Front Command guidance at [oref.org.il](https://www.oref.org.il/eng)).

**Book the busy trades now.** Before Pesach especially, cleaners, kashering services, handymen and AC technicians fill up early. Get written quotes with a clear scope, for example "deep clean, oven and fridge, windows and shutters, balcony", and agree that you will receive photos when each job is finished.

## 4 weeks before: fix and plan

- **Repairs.** Approve and schedule anything found at inspection. Leave a buffer for a second visit if a part needs ordering.
- **Utilities.** Confirm the electricity and water accounts are active and paid, and that the gas supply (central or cylinders) is working and safe. A first-time arrival is not when you want to discover a disconnected meter.
- **Internet and TV.** If you suspended the service, reactivate it now. Activation can take days.
- **Arnona and vaad bayit.** Make sure both are paid up. Municipal and building payments can be harder to settle from abroad if a foreign card is declined.
- **Building matters.** Ask the vaad bayit about any works planned around your dates (scaffolding, elevator maintenance, water shut-offs) and about shared-space rules for the sukkah if you are coming for Sukkot.

## 2 weeks before: comfort and kashrut

- **Deep clean** after repairs, not before. Dust from a repair undoes a clean.
- **For Pesach:** schedule the kashering of the kitchen, or the switch to Pesach dishes and covering of work surfaces, in the order your rav or family practice requires. Agree what is done before you arrive and what you will do yourself.
- **For Sukkot:** check the sukkah frame, panels and schach are complete and stored where you think they are, or book assembly.
- **Linens and basics:** fresh bedding and towels, toilet paper, dish soap, light bulbs, batteries for remotes and smoke detectors.
- **Pest control** if the apartment has been closed for a long time, timed so the apartment can be aired before you arrive.

## 72 hours before: the arrival touches

- AC switched on to the right temperature (cooling for Tishrei, heating for a cold spring evening), and the water heater on.
- Run every tap and flush every toilet to clear stagnant water from the pipes.
- Fridge stocked with a few basics: milk, bread, water, fruit, plus Shabbat or chag essentials if you land close to the start.
- Keys: confirm who hands them over and the backup plan for a late-night arrival.
- A final photo set, so you know what you will find.

## When you leave again: close up properly

The weeks after the chag are when damage quietly starts in an empty home. Before you fly back:

1. **Water.** Many owners close the apartment's main water valve as a precaution against leaks. Check first how this affects the water heater, the solar system and any balcony irrigation, and ask a plumber if unsure.
2. **Electricity.** Leave the fridge on or off deliberately: if off, empty it and leave the door ajar to prevent mould. Unplug non-essential appliances, and consider timers for a light or two.
3. **Air and damp.** Leave interior doors open, and consider a dehumidifier on a timer in damp-prone apartments.
4. **Mail and notices.** Arrange collection. Municipal letters arrive in Hebrew and some have deadlines.
5. **Neighbours and vaad.** Leave a local contact number for emergencies.
6. **Insurance.** Check whether your home policy requires periodic inspections or limits cover after a certain number of days unoccupied [DA VERIFICARE: wording varies by insurer; check your policy].

## A word on arnona for empty months

Owners often ask whether they can stop paying arnona between visits. A temporary exemption exists for property that is **genuinely empty and unused**, often up to six months and usually once per ownership, on application to the municipality ([IsraelNonResident](https://israelnonresident.com/qa/arnona-exemption-empty-israeli-apartment-non-resident/)). Some municipalities require the property to be unfurnished, so a furnished holiday home you visit regularly is unlikely to qualify. Jerusalem lists its residential discounts on the [municipality website](https://www.jerusalem.muni.il/he/residents/arnona/arnonadiscounts/apartments/) [DA VERIFICARE: Jerusalem conditions for the empty-property exemption].

## Who should do all this?

Owners usually choose one of three set-ups, and each has trade-offs:

- **Family or a friendly neighbour.** It costs nothing, but favours run out, and it is awkward to ask someone to chase a technician three times or to judge whether a damp patch is serious.
- **Individual tradespeople you coordinate yourself.** This works if you have reliable contacts and time to manage them across time zones. The weak point is the gap between "the cleaner didn't show" and finding a replacement two days before the chag.
- **A property manager.** One point of contact who inspects, books, supervises and reports with photos, at a monthly or per-visit cost. The value is less in any single task than in having someone accountable for the whole sequence.

Whichever you choose, put the plan in writing: who has keys, who can approve spending and up to what amount, and how you will receive updates.

## The year-round habit that makes holidays easy

The best holiday preparation happens between holidays. A regular inspection, every two weeks or monthly depending on the building and season, catches a slow leak before it becomes a ceiling repair, and keeps bills and mail under control. It also means the pre-holiday visit is a check, not a rescue.

## Your pre-holiday checklist

- [ ] Arrival date fixed and countdown set.
- [ ] Inspection with photos or video done 8 weeks out.
- [ ] Cleaner, kashering and AC service booked with written scope.
- [ ] Repairs approved, with a buffer for parts.
- [ ] Utilities, internet, arnona and vaad bayit confirmed.
- [ ] Linens, basics and a small grocery stock arranged.
- [ ] Key handover and late-arrival plan confirmed.
- [ ] Close-up plan for departure (water, fridge, mail, contacts).
