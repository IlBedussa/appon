> **Not tax advice.** This article is a general overview for owners who live outside Israel. Tax outcomes depend on your residence, your other income and property, your country's rules and any treaty. Always confirm with a qualified Israeli tax adviser, and with an adviser in your country of residence, before choosing a route.

Many owners abroad assume that if they do not live in Israel, Israeli tax on their rent is either automatic or optional. It is neither. Below is a map of how it works, the documents involved and the mistakes we see most often. It draws on professional sources and Tax Authority material, and cites Dray & Dray, a Jerusalem accounting firm that writes extensively for English speakers.

## The starting point: rent is Israeli-source income

Rent from a property located in Israel is income produced in Israel under the source rules of the Income Tax Ordinance. It is therefore taxable in Israel **even if the landlord is not an Israeli resident** ([Melnik](https://www.melnik.org.il/en/withholding-rent-foreign-resident-israel/)). Your country of residence may also tax it, which is where treaties and foreign tax credits come in (see below).

## The three routes for residential rent

For an individual letting an apartment for residential use, there are three routes. The choice can be made each year ([Melnik](https://www.melnik.org.il/en/oleh-buying-apartment-israel-purchase-tax-rent-sale/); [Dray & Dray](https://cpa-dray.com/en/blog/tax-on-rental-income/)).

### 1. The exemption route

- Full exemption on residential rent **up to ₪5,654 per month** (2026), tapering to nil at **₪11,308**. For every shekel above the ceiling, a shekel of exemption is lost.
- The test is monthly, against your **total** residential rent from all apartments.
- The tenant must be an individual (or a company approved to house its employees), and the apartment must be let for residential use.
- Dray & Dray reports that the ceiling is **frozen until 2027** under the 2025 Economic Arrangements Law, even as rents rise.

**For non-residents:** IsraelNonResident notes that Israeli practice has generally allowed non-residents to use the exemption for an apartment let for residential purposes, but owning other property, including abroad, can affect eligibility ([source](https://israelnonresident.com/tax/non-resident-taxation/israeli-rental-income-tax-tracks-non-residents/)) [DA VERIFICARE: confirm current Tax Authority position for foreign residents].

### 2. The flat 10% route (section 122)

- **10% of gross rent** from the first shekel.
- **No** deduction for expenses, interest or depreciation.
- Paid within **30 days of the end of the tax year**, which in practice means by 30 January.
- Landlords without a tax file can open one for this purpose using **Form 3302**. Melnik notes that the conditions and ceiling for being relieved from an annual return should be checked for foreign residents ([Melnik](https://www.melnik.org.il/en/withholding-rent-foreign-resident-israel/)) [DA VERIFICARE: Form 3302 conditions and ceiling for foreign residents].
- In 2026 the Tax Authority announced an online system for reporting and paying tax on rental income, including the 10% route ([Execution Instruction 5/2026](https://www.capitax.co.il/Attachments/5-2026.pdf)). According to Melnik, the instruction does not address foreign residents.

IsraelNonResident warns that missing the 30-day window can cost you the 10% rate for that year, with reassessment at marginal rates plus interest ([source](https://israelnonresident.com/tax/non-resident-taxation/israeli-rental-income-tax-tracks-non-residents/)).

### 3. The marginal (ordinary) route

- Rent is taxed at ordinary rates **after deducting expenses**: mortgage interest on the purchase loan, repairs, management fees, and depreciation (generally 2% a year on the building).
- For income not from personal exertion, rates start at **31%**, and from age 60 the entry rate is 10% ([Melnik](https://www.melnik.org.il/en/oleh-buying-apartment-israel-purchase-tax-rent-sale/)) [DA VERIFICARE: how the age-60 rule applies to foreign residents].
- An annual return is required.
- Dray & Dray's worked example shows how, with a large mortgage and depreciation, this route can produce little or no taxable income ([Dray & Dray](https://cpa-dray.com/en/blog/tax-on-rental-income/)).

## The capital gains link people forget

Your choice today affects tax when you sell. Under the exemption and 10% routes, Melnik explains that on a later sale without a full mas shevach exemption, **2% depreciation for each year let is added to the gain whether or not it was claimed** ([Melnik](https://www.melnik.org.il/en/oleh-buying-apartment-israel-purchase-tax-rent-sale/)). Dray & Dray reports a recent ruling (Reshef Chen) suggesting that landlords on the *exemption* route who did not deduct depreciation need not add it back ([Dray & Dray](https://cpa-dray.com/en/blog/tax-on-rental-income/)). The two sources differ on this point, so get advice if a sale is on the horizon [DA VERIFICARE: status of the Reshef Chen ruling].

## Withholding by the tenant

- A **private tenant** renting a home to live in cannot deduct the rent as a business expense, so the payment is generally outside the order that triggers **35%** withholding on rent (Income Tax Circular 5/98, as described by [Melnik](https://www.melnik.org.il/en/withholding-rent-foreign-resident-israel/)).
- A **business tenant** (for example, a company housing staff) must withhold 35% unless the assessing officer approves a lower rate under regulation 4. It is best to apply before payments begin.
- Section 170 provides for 25% withholding on certain payments to foreign residents. Melnik notes it is **not settled** whether it applies to a private tenant paying a foreign landlord, and suggests raising it with the assessing officer in advance [DA VERIFICARE].

## Getting the money out: Form 2513

Before an Israeli bank sends money to a foreign resident, it ordinarily wants either a certificate from the assessing officer or a declaration. Rent is not among the payment types on the bank declaration form (2513/2). So the practical route is **Form 2513**, an application to the assessing officer, filed with the lease, ideally **before the first rent payment** ([Melnik](https://www.melnik.org.il/en/withholding-rent-foreign-resident-israel/)).

Banks are also under pressure to prevent tax evasion. Dray & Dray describes cases where banks asked for proof of reporting before accepting rental deposits ([Dray & Dray](https://cpa-dray.com/en/blog/reporting-exempt-rental-income/)). Keep your filings and receipts organised.

## Filing obligations

According to the Tax Authority guide (as cited by [Melnik](https://www.melnik.org.il/en/withholding-rent-foreign-resident-israel/)), a foreign resident with Israeli rental income is exempt from filing a return **if tax was actually withheld** from that income. If nothing was withheld, which is common with private tenants, you need to establish what applies to you. A return is also how you recover excess withholding. The Tax Authority's own guide is published on [gov.il](https://www.gov.il/en/pages/rental-income-guide) [DA VERIFICARE: gov.il page blocked automated access; confirm URL and wording].

## Treaties: they rarely reduce the Israeli tax

Under the OECD model's immovable-property article, rent may be taxed in the country where the property is located, with no rate cap. So a treaty generally **does not reduce** Israeli tax on Israeli rent. It mainly affects whether and how your country of residence taxes it and gives credit. Melnik notes, for example, that under the Israel–France treaty such income is taxable only in Israel, while under the Israel–UK treaty it *may* be taxed in Israel ([Melnik](https://www.melnik.org.il/en/withholding-rent-foreign-resident-israel/)). Check your own treaty, and how the Israeli route you pick interacts with your home-country credit, as IsraelNonResident points out ([source](https://israelnonresident.com/tax/non-resident-taxation/israeli-rental-income-tax-tracks-non-residents/)).

## Short-term rentals are a different animal

Airbnb-style lettings are treated as **business income**. They cannot use the exemption or the 10% route, and may bring National Insurance and VAT obligations ([Dray & Dray](https://cpa-dray.com/en/blog/short-term-rentals-airbnb-israeli-taxation/)).

| What | When |
|---|---|

| 10% payment | Within 30 days of year end (by 30 January) |
| Form 2513 / withholding reduction | Before the first rent payment |
| Annual return (marginal route, or when required) | Per Tax Authority deadlines [DA VERIFICARE: filing deadline for foreign residents] |

## Common mistakes we see

1. Assuming "I'm below the ceiling, so nothing applies," without checking total rent across all apartments or eligibility as a non-resident.
2. Missing the 30 January deadline on the 10% route.
3. Starting the lease before sorting out Form 2513, then finding the bank will not transfer.
4. Forgetting the mas shevach effect of the route chosen.
5. Not keeping receipts and statements organised for two tax systems.
