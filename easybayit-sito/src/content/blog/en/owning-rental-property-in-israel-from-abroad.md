Owning an apartment in Israel while living in New York, London, Paris or Milan is more common than many people think. In 2025 foreign residents bought 684 homes in Jerusalem alone, plus 186 in Tel Aviv, 169 in Netanya and 137 in Beit Shemesh, according to Ministry of Finance figures reported by [Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-strong-shekel-hits-home-purchases-by-foreign-residents-1001548068). Buying is the exciting part. Owning it from 3,000 or 9,000 kilometres away is where the real work starts.

This guide covers the whole picture: what you pay when you buy, how rent is taxed, what the law expects from a landlord, and what running the apartment involves day to day. It is general information, not legal or tax advice. Rules change, and your own situation matters, so check the specifics with an Israeli lawyer and accountant.

## 1. Before you buy: the taxes that shape your numbers

**Purchase tax (mas rechisha).** A buyer who is not an Israeli tax resident does not get the reduced "single dwelling" brackets. According to [David Melnik Law Offices](https://www.melnik.org.il/en/israel-property-tax-non-residents/), a foreign resident pays 8% on the price up to ₪6,055,070 and 10% above that, under brackets frozen from 16 January 2025 to 15 January 2028. On a ₪3,000,000 apartment that means ₪240,000 of purchase tax. Dray & Dray publishes [an English overview of the brackets](https://cpa-dray.com/en/blog/purchase-tax-rates-israel/) and an English calculator, which is useful for a first estimate.

**Capital gains tax (mas shevach) when you sell.** The tax on the real (inflation-adjusted) gain is 25% for individuals, foreign residents included, with a linear relief for qualifying apartments bought before 2014 ([Melnik](https://www.melnik.org.il/en/israel-property-tax-non-residents/)). A foreign resident may in some cases claim the single-apartment exemption. As [Dray & Dray explains in Hebrew](https://cpa-dray.com/he/blog/mas-shevach-inherited-property-foreign-resident/), the conditions include owning only that apartment in Israel, holding it for at least 18 months, and bringing written confirmation from the tax authority of your country of residence that you own no home there.

**What this means for you:** buying as a foreign resident costs more upfront, and how you rent the apartment affects your eventual exit tax (more on this below). Bring these figures into your yield calculation from day one. Nefesh B'Nefesh notes that gross residential yields in Israel are typically [in the 2–4% range](https://www.nbn.org.il/life-in-israel/finances/investments/real-estate-investing-in-israel/), so transaction taxes weigh heavily on returns.

## 2. Long-term rental, holiday home, or both?

Overseas owners usually fall into one of three patterns:

- **Long-term rental.** A tenant on a 12-month (or longer) lease. This gives the steadiest income and the most legal structure.
- **Family or holiday home.** The apartment stays empty most of the year and is used for Tishrei, Pesach or summer visits. There is no income, but the costs and risks are real: leaks, mail, bills and arnona.
- **Short-term rental.** Airbnb-style letting. Under Israeli tax practice this is treated as business activity, not passive rent. [Dray & Dray](https://cpa-dray.com/en/blog/short-term-rentals-airbnb-israeli-taxation/) notes that it is excluded from the 10% track and the exemption, and may bring National Insurance and VAT obligations. Municipal rules can apply too.

Most of this guide focuses on long-term rentals, which is where most of the legal and tax questions arise.

## 4. Taxes on the rent: three routes

Rent from an Israeli apartment is Israeli-source income, so it is taxable in Israel wherever you live ([Melnik](https://www.melnik.org.il/en/withholding-rent-foreign-resident-israel/)). For residential lettings, individuals generally choose between three routes, described by [Dray & Dray](https://cpa-dray.com/en/blog/tax-on-rental-income/):

1. **Exemption.** Full exemption up to ₪5,654 of rent per month, tapering to zero at ₪11,308. Dray & Dray reports that the ceiling is frozen until 2027.
2. **Flat 10% of gross rent.** No expenses or depreciation can be deducted, and the payment is due within 30 days of the end of the tax year.
3. **Marginal rates on net income.** Expenses such as mortgage interest, repairs and depreciation can be deducted.

Two practical points catch overseas owners off guard. First, getting the money out: Israeli banks usually want a certificate or declaration before transferring rent to a foreign resident, and Melnik explains that the practical route for rent is an application on **Form 2513** to the assessing officer, *before* the first payment. Second, the exit: under the exemption and 10% routes, the depreciation you could have claimed is added back when mas shevach is calculated on a sale without a full exemption. Our separate overview of non-resident rental taxes goes deeper, and it is worth reading before you sign a lease.

## 5. The landlord's legal duties (Fair Rental Law)

Since 2017, residential leases are governed by a dedicated chapter of the Rental and Borrowing Law, often called the Fair Rental Law. Most of its rules cannot be waived to the tenant's detriment. The key ones, from the [consolidated law text](https://www.nevo.co.il/law_html/law00/5149.htm):

- The lease must be in writing and include set details, including known defects (s. 25B–25C).
- The apartment must be fit for living: drainage, electricity, ventilation and natural light, drinking water, a lockable entrance door, and no unreasonable safety risk (s. 25F and First Schedule).
- Repairs: up to **30 days** from the tenant's request, or **3 days** for an urgent defect that prevents reasonable living (s. 25H).
- Deposit/guarantees: capped at the lower of **three months' rent** or rent for **one third of the lease term** (s. 25J).
- The tenant pays rent, arnona, utilities and routine vaad bayit. The landlord pays building insurance, upgrades to fixed systems, and a broker the landlord hired (s. 25I).

For an owner abroad, the 3-day rule is the one that matters most. A burst pipe on a Friday afternoon needs someone local with a plumber's number and the authority to approve the job.

## 6. When things go wrong: arrears and eviction

Eviction always requires a court order. [IsraelNonResident](https://israelnonresident.com/property/rental-management/evicting-tenants-israel-non-resident-landlord/), a law site for non-resident landlords, estimates roughly 3–6 months for an uncontested case and 9–18 months when the tenant contests. Prevention is cheaper than cure: check tenants carefully, take a guarantee within the legal cap, keep a clear payment record, and act on the first missed payment rather than the third.

## 7. Running costs and the empty-apartment question

Budget for:

- **Arnona** (municipal tax), paid by the tenant during a lease and by the owner when the apartment is empty.
- **Vaad bayit** (building committee), routine dues usually paid by the tenant; special levies for capital works fall on the owner.
- **Insurance**: structure insurance is the owner's cost under the law; contents and third-party cover should be checked with your insurer, especially for empty periods [DA VERIFICARE: vacancy clauses vary by policy].
- **Maintenance reserve** for boilers, air conditioning and plumbing.

An apartment that is genuinely empty and unused may qualify for a temporary arnona exemption, often up to six months and usually only once during your ownership, according to [IsraelNonResident](https://israelnonresident.com/qa/arnona-exemption-empty-israeli-apartment-non-resident/). You have to apply to the municipality, and some cities require the unit to be unfurnished. Check your city's rules before you count on it [DA VERIFICARE: Jerusalem-specific conditions].

## 8. Managing from abroad: what actually has to happen

Whether you do it yourself or hire help, someone needs to:

1. Market the apartment, screen tenants and sign a compliant lease (a licensed broker if a finder's fee is charged).
2. Collect rent, follow up on late payments, and keep records for your accountant.
3. Take maintenance calls, get quotes, approve work and check it was done.
4. Transfer arnona, water and electricity accounts at each change of tenant.
5. Inspect the apartment periodically and at move-out, with photos.
6. Handle the deposit return within the legal deadline (60 days after the apartment is returned, or when the tenant's debts are settled, whichever is later; s. 25J(e)).

Time zones, Shabbat and Hebrew-only paperwork are what make this hard from abroad, not any single task.

## A practical checklist

- [ ] Get a tax adviser's view on the track (exemption / 10% / marginal) *before* the lease starts.
- [ ] Prepare a Fair Rental Law-compliant lease with a clear list of defects and inventory.
- [ ] Arrange Form 2513 and your bank's requirements before the first rent payment.
- [ ] Decide who in Israel has authority to approve urgent repairs, and up to what amount.
- [ ] Set a schedule for inspections and for an annual statement for your accountant.
