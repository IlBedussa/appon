"What should I ask for it?" is the first question almost every Jerusalem owner asks us, and with good reason. Price it too high and the apartment sits empty through the busy season. Price it too low and you give away money every month for the length of the lease. The good news is that Jerusalem has solid official data to start from. Here is how to use it.

## Start with the official numbers

Israel's Central Bureau of Statistics (CBS) publishes average monthly rents by city and apartment size every quarter. The CBS website was not reachable by our tools, so the figures below come from the CBS series as republished by [CEIC](https://www.ceicdata.com/en/israel/average-rental-price-dwellings/rental-prices-avg-jerusalem), checked on 2 October 2026. Please confirm them against the [CBS table](https://www.cbs.gov.il/en/subjects/Pages/Average-Monthly-Prices-of-Rent.aspx) before reusing them [DA VERIFICARE: cross-check with the original CBS table].

**Jerusalem, average monthly rent (quarter ending June 2026):**

| Apartment size (Israeli room count) | Average rent |
|---|---|
| Up to 2 rooms | ₪3,800 |
| 2.5–3 rooms | ₪4,890 |
| 3.5–4 rooms | ₪6,180 |
| 4.5–6 rooms | ₪7,748 |
| **All sizes** | **₪5,347** |

*Source: CBS via [CEIC](https://www.ceicdata.com/en/israel/average-rental-price-dwellings), rounded.*

For context, the national average for the same quarter was ₪5,085 ([CBS via CEIC](https://www.ceicdata.com/en/israel/average-rental-price-dwellings/rental-prices-avg)). Jerusalem also rose from ₪5,315 in the previous quarter (CBS revises recent quarters; the first published Q1 2026 figure was ₪5,280). Earlier in the year, [Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/real-estate/article/hjekp6kgzx), citing CBS, reported that national rents were up 3.5% year on year in Q1 2026.

**A note on room counts:** Israeli listings count the living room as a room, so a "3-room" apartment usually has two bedrooms. Analysts commonly treat the 2.5–3 room band as the closest match to a two-bedroom apartment ([Sands of Wealth](https://sandsofwealth.com/blogs/news/jerusalem-rents)).

## How Jerusalem compares with nearby markets

If you are deciding where to buy, or just want perspective, the Q1 2026 CBS averages reported by [Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/real-estate/article/hjekp6kgzx) show a wide spread. Tel Aviv was at ₪7,351, Jerusalem ₪5,280, Beit Shemesh ₪4,782, Netanya ₪4,729 and Haifa ₪3,384, against a national average of ₪5,027. Jerusalem sits a little above the national figure but well below Tel Aviv. Within the city, the gap between neighbourhoods can be as large as the gap between cities, which is why local comparables matter more than any headline number.

## Why your apartment is not "average"

An average combines Rehavia and Pisgat Ze'ev, renovated and tired, ground floor and penthouse. Use the CBS band for your size as an anchor, then adjust for the features tenants actually pay for:

- **Location and access.** Walking distance to the light rail, the centre, universities or popular synagogues and schools.
- **Building.** An elevator (important for families and older tenants), parking, a storage room, and a well-run vaad bayit.
- **Safety.** A mamad (safe room) in the apartment or easy shelter access has become a priority for many renters.
- **Outdoor space.** A balcony, especially one that works as a sukkah, matters to many Jerusalem families.
- **Condition.** Renovated kitchen and bathroom, working air conditioning in every room, decent windows and no damp.
- **Furnished or not.** Furnished units appeal to students, academics, diplomats and relocating families, who are often on shorter leases.

There is no official price for each feature. Any figure you see (for example, "parking adds ₪X") is someone's estimate, not a statistic. The reliable way to value features is to compare apartments that are similar in everything except that feature.

## A five-step method to set your asking rent

1. **Anchor.** Take the CBS average for your size band from the table above.
2. **Compare.** Find 8–10 comparable listings on the main portals: same neighbourhood, similar size and floor, similar condition. Note asking rents and how long each has been online.
3. **Adjust.** Move up or down for the features above. Be honest about condition: tenants compare photos ruthlessly.
4. **Test.** List at your chosen price and track the response in the first 10–14 days: enquiries, viewings and serious applications.
5. **Review.** Plenty of viewings but no applications usually means a condition or price problem. Few enquiries usually means the price or the photos.

Official averages describe the rented homes in CBS's survey, not only new listings, so current asking prices for well-presented apartments can differ from the average [DA VERIFICARE: CBS methodology note, new leases vs. renewals].

## The real cost of an empty month

Every month without a tenant costs about 8.3% of a year's rent (1 month out of 12). On an apartment at the Jerusalem average of ₪5,347, one empty month equals ₪5,347 that no later increase recovers quickly. If holding out for ₪200 more per month costs you an extra empty month, it takes more than two years of the higher rent to break even (5,347 ÷ 200 ≈ 27 months). In many cases, pricing to rent within two to four weeks is the better financial decision.

## What the law means for your pricing

The Fair Rental Law (2017 amendment to the Rental and Borrowing Law) does not cap rent, but it shapes how you set and change it ([consolidated law](https://www.nevo.co.il/law_html/law00/5149.htm)):

- **Deposit cap.** Monetary guarantees are limited to the lower of three months' rent or one third of the lease term's rent (s. 25J). A higher rent raises the cap in proportion, but a "six months' deposit" is not allowed.
- **No mid-lease surprises.** Rent stays as written unless the lease includes an indexation or step-up clause, so decide on CPI-linking *before* signing.
- **Renewals.** If the landlord holds an option to extend, notice must be given at least 90 days before the end of the lease, and the extension terms must have been set in advance in the contract (s. 25L).
- **Who pays what.** The tenant pays arnona, utilities and routine vaad bayit (s. 25I). Building insurance and capital upgrades stay with the owner, so build them into your numbers rather than into the rent line.

## Rent and tax: an often-missed link

For individuals, residential rent up to ₪5,654 per month can be fully exempt, with the exemption phasing out up to ₪11,308. As [Dray & Dray](https://cpa-dray.com/en/blog/tax-on-rental-income/) points out, the ceiling has been frozen until 2027 even as rents rise. With the Jerusalem average at around ₪5,350, many single-apartment owners sit close to that line. Your total rental income across all apartments counts, and non-residents should check eligibility and filing rules with an adviser [DA VERIFICARE: exemption eligibility for foreign residents with property abroad]. ## What about management costs?

If you use a manager, fees in Israel are commonly quoted at about 7–10% of the rent plus VAT. On the Jerusalem average rent, 8% would be about ₪428 a month before 18% VAT, or roughly ₪505 including it. That is a cost to compare against the time saved and the reduction in empty months.
